# Usability test script — "The Handover Problem" scenario

For **n = 3–5 adult participants** (any adult works; at least one who has managed people is ideal). 45 minutes each, remote or in person. This protocol was prepared for the portfolio owner to run; no sessions have taken place yet (see `evaluation/METHODS.md`).

Before any session: obtain consent (see `consent-text.md`), and confirm the participant can use a computer with a keyboard. Record audio/screen only with consent. Collect no personal data beyond a first name or pseudonym.

## Setup (5 min)

- Open `docs/05-build/scenario/index.html` in the participant's browser, maximized.
- Say: "This is a draft training scenario. I didn't build it to be easy — I want to find what's confusing. There are no wrong answers and nothing you do here is being graded."
- Note-taking template: `findings-template.md`.

## Task 1 — Play through once (10 min, silent observation)

Say only: "Play through this. Take the choices you'd actually make."

Observe and timestamp (do not interrupt):

- Where they hover or hesitate before choosing
- Whether they read all three options before choosing
- Any misclicks, re-reads, or scrolling confusion
- Their reaction at each consequence scene (nod / frown / surprise)
- The ending they reach and their immediate reaction to the debrief

## Task 2 — Think-aloud second run (15 min)

"Run it again, this time talking me through what you're thinking as you read each option. Choose differently where you like."

For each of the 3 decision points, probe (only if not already covered):

1. "Read me the three options in your own words."
2. "Which of these would be hardest to actually say in real life?"
3. "Did any option feel like the 'right answer'? Which one, and what gave it away?"
4. (At the consequence scenes) "What did this tell you about your choice?"
5. (At the debrief) "Does this match what just happened to you?"

## Task 3 — Debrief interview (10 min)

1. "Was the situation realistic? Have you been in anything like it?" (probe for the closest real example)
2. "Was any choice obviously the right one? Obviously wrong?"
3. "What did the conversation with Priya change, if anything?"
4. "Was anything confusing, unrealistic, or annoying — wording, length, layout?"
5. "How did you find the choices — clicking, keyboard? Any trouble seeing or focusing anything?"
6. "If your manager had to do this as training, would it be useful? What's missing?"
7. "Rate: how likely would you be to recommend this to another new manager, 0–10?" (record the number)

## Job aid add-on (5 min, optional)

Open `docs/05-build/job-aid/index.html`: "You're about to walk into a difficult feedback conversation; you have this page. What do you do first?" Note whether they can locate the structure and the defensive-responses section without prompting.

## Closing (2 min)

Explain what happens next, re-offer the deletion option for recordings, thank them. Pay/compensation per the organization's policy.

## Analysis

- Code observations against: realism, choice difficulty, obviousness of options, wording problems, navigation/interaction issues, emotional response to consequences.
- Log **every** finding in `findings-template.md` with a severity and the revision it triggers (or why none is needed).
- **Revision rule:** findings rated high or medium require a documented revision before publish; the revision ledger records what changed and where.
