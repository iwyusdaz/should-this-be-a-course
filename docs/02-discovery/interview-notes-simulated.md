# Interview notes — simulated

> **Simulated data.** These six interviews are fictional, constructed for portfolio purposes. They were written to be realistically messy — incomplete sentences, contradictions, and one thread that does not fit the main pattern. All names are fictional.

**Method note:** in a real engagement these would be transcribed recordings, coded against the causal buckets (environment, process, skill, motivation/consequences, other). Here the notes are presented as analysis-ready summaries; the coding appears in the [root-cause analysis](../03-analysis/root-cause-bem.html).

## Interview 1 — Manager M1 (new manager)

Clinic: Birchwood. Role: Nurse Unit Manager. Promoted 8 months ago from senior nurse; 14 direct reports. Previously a peer of most of her team.

> "Honestly? I haven't done one-on-ones. I keep meaning to. There's no slot in my week where it fits — I'm on the floor 90% of my shift, and when I'm not, I'm doing rosters and incident reports.
>
> The last time I gave someone feedback… it was about Priya's handovers, actually — no, wait, that's not me, that's the Harborview side. Sorry, the last time was Jordan, he was double-booking follow-ups. I told him at the desk, in front of the admin assistant, 'you've got to fix the bookings.' He was fine about it. I think? It's hard, they all used to be my colleagues.
>
> Training? I got two days of orientation: finance systems, incident reporting, the award and rostering rules. Nothing about managing people. I asked if there was a management course and there's a waitlist for next year.
>
> What am I measured on? Patients per day, wait times, incident closure rate. Roster cost. Team development — no, that's not on it."

**Coded:** environment (no time/space for 1:1s), skill (avoids hard conversations; gave public, vague feedback), motivation (scorecard has no team-development items).

## Interview 2 — Manager M2 (experienced manager)

Clinic: Fairhaven. Role: Clinic Manager, 11 years in role. 13 direct reports.

> "I do meet everyone, every week. Quick stand-up style, five minutes each while I do my rounds. We go through the day's list, any leave, any equipment problems.
>
> Feedback? I give feedback constantly. Constantly. If something's wrong I say it there and then.
>
> [Shown the pulse data: his team's 'useful feedback' scores are among the lowest, 2.1/5] — huh. Well. Maybe 'useful' is the wrong word. If the patient flow is the problem, what do people want from me, a pep talk?
>
> Reviews are fine, we do them every year, it's a form on the intranet, mostly ratings, takes twenty minutes.
>
> Measured on: wait times, throughput, budget variance. Same as every clinic manager."

**Coded:** environment/process (meetings exist but are task round-ups, not feedback), skill (equates task direction with feedback), motivation (throughput metrics). **Contradiction noted:** believes he gives constant feedback; his team's survey scores disagree. This contradiction is a finding, not noise: the *form* of his meetings doesn't match what staff experience as feedback.

## Interview 3 — Manager M3 (overloaded span)

Clinic: Kestrel. Role: Allied Health Manager. 18 direct reports across two sites (high end of the org's spans).

> "Eighteen. Eighteen reports and two sites. You do the math on a weekly one-on-one — that's a part-time job. I meet with people when something breaks, honestly.
>
> The new grads are the ones who struggle and the ones who leave. We had two leave inside three months this year. Both said the same thing in exit — 'no idea how I was doing'. I wasn't surprised. Nobody sits with them.
>
> There's supposed to be a policy about one-on-ones? I've never seen it. [HR later confirmed a policy exists; it says meetings are 'encouraged'.]
>
> If I had a template, a slot in the calendar, and maybe eight fewer reports… the template part, that's easy. The other two, that's you asking the COO for things."

**Coded:** environment (span of control, no time), process (no cadence/template), skill (knows what good looks like — can describe it — but cannot do it at current span), motivation (n/a — willing).

## Interview 4 — Staff S1 (nurse, 2 years)

Clinic: Harborview. Role: Registered Nurse.

> "Feedback — like, actual feedback? [laughs] The review form, once a year. She reads out my ratings, I initial it. There's a comments box but it's optional and nobody writes in it, so.
>
> Day to day, you hear about it if something's wrong, usually second-hand. 'Word is the handovers are slipping.' From who? From everyone, from the group chat.
>
> The useful feedback I've gotten here came from the clinical educator, not my manager — she watched me do an ECG once and told me exactly what I did well and what to fix. Took ten minutes. Why can't it be like that?
>
> Would I stay? My friend left Betterton [another fictional clinic] because she felt invisible. I'm not there yet. The 2pm-to-10pm agency across town pays the same and they do monthly check-ins. Actually they do."

**Coded:** process (annual review is the only formal point), environment (no 1:1 structure), motivation for managers implied. Retention risk linked to feedback in the staff member's own words.

## Interview 5 — Staff S2 (new hire, 6 weeks in)

Clinic: Dockside. Role: Medical Assistant.

> "Six weeks in. Nobody's sat me down, like, properly. My buddy on the floor shows me stuff, which is great, but there's no 'here's how you're doing' conversation. I asked my manager in week two if we could do a check-in and she said 'let's wait until you're through compliance training', and that's next month sometime?
>
> At my last job we had a thirty-day review, sixty-day, ninety-day. Here I genuinely don't know if I'm doing well. I like the team. I'm keeping my options open. Don't put my name in anything."

**Coded:** environment/process (no onboarding feedback structure), links directly to the 90-day turnover metric.

## Interview 6 — HR business partner (HR1)

> "The policy — yes, there's a one-on-one policy. Two pages, 2022, it says managers 'are encouraged to hold regular one-on-one meetings'. 'Regular' is never defined, there's no template, and nothing is tracked. It was written before the expansion, when the average manager had six or seven reports.
>
> The review form: five competency ratings, one optional comments box. We've talked about revising it for two years. It needs the ops directors to agree, and they won't without the COO.
>
> The email series in 2024 — we bought a content pack, sent six emails. Open rate was tracked, that's all. Something like 30% opened the first one.
>
> What would I fix? The scorecards. While 'team development' is worth nothing on a manager's scorecard and throughput is worth everything, you're asking people to do things their job doesn't ask them to do. But scorecards are set by operations, not HR. That's a COO conversation, and nobody's had it.
>
> One thing that doesn't fit, and you should know: Cedar Point. Small teams, a manager who does weekly one-on-ones, everyone likes her — and their feedback scores are just as bad. I don't know what that's about. Maybe worth a look before you blame everything on spans of control."

**Coded:** process (policy vague, untracked), motivation (scorecard misalignment — HR names it directly), plus the **doesn't-fit thread**: Cedar Point, which also appears in the survey analysis.

## Cross-interview signals

| Signal | Where it shows up |
|---|---|
| One-on-ones are rare and unstructured | M1 (none), M2 (task round-ups), M3 (none), S1, S2, HR1 (policy vague) |
| Annual review is the only formal feedback point, ratings only | M2, S1, HR1 |
| New managers get no people-management support | M1, HR1 |
| Managers are measured on throughput, not team development | M1, M2, M3, HR1 |
| Feedback absence is named by staff as a reason to leave | S1, S2, M3 (exit anecdotes) |
| Meeting ≠ feedback: structure and content matter | M2 contradiction, HR1's Cedar Point thread |
| Span of control has crept up | M3 (18), HR1 (policy written for 6–7), org chart review |
