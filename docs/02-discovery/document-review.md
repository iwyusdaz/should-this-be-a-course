# Document review — annual review form, one-on-one policy, org chart, scorecards

> **Simulated documents.** The four documents below are fictional, constructed for portfolio purposes to represent the artifacts a Brightline-style discovery would collect. Quoted text is invented.

What a person *can* do is shaped by the forms, policies and scorecards around them. Four documents were reviewed in full; the findings feed directly into the [root-cause analysis](../03-analysis/root-cause-bem.html).

## Document 1: Annual performance review form (2021, v3)

**What it contains:**
- Five competency ratings (1–5): Clinical Quality, Teamwork, Compliance, Communication, Professionalism.
- One optional free-text box: "Additional comments (optional)."
- No goals section. No employee self-assessment. No space for examples or evidence — ratings only.
- Signature lines for manager and employee ("acknowledgement of discussion").

**Analysis:**
- The form asks for judgments, not observations. A manager cannot record *what happened* anywhere; there is no prompt for behavior, impact, or an agreed next step.
- The single feedback field is optional, which the form's own analytics confirm is used less than 20% of the time (HR's 2025 form-usage report).
- As the only formal feedback point of the year (interviews S1, M2; policy below), this form structurally caps what "formal feedback" can be at Brightline: a set of numbers.

**Implication (process cause):** the review form only asks for ratings — one of the four root causes.

## Document 2: One-on-one meeting policy (2022, HR-P-014)

**Full substance, quoted:**
> "Managers are encouraged to hold regular one-on-one meetings with their direct reports to discuss workload, wellbeing and development."

**Analysis:**
- That is the entire operative text. "Encouraged", "regular", and "one-on-one meetings" are all undefined. No cadence (weekly? monthly?), no minimum duration, no agenda or template, no tracking, no owner.
- Written in 2022, when the median span of control was 6–7 reports; spans have since crept to a median of 13 (org chart extract below). The policy assumes a world that no longer exists.
- Not referenced in manager onboarding (interview M1 had never seen it), not in the LMS, not tracked in any dashboard.

**Implication (environment cause):** one-on-ones aren't scheduled; there is no template and no time allocated.

## Document 3: Org chart and span-of-control extract (HRIS, 2026-09)

**What it shows:**
- 70 people managers; 12 regional clinics; ~900 staff total.
- Median span of control: **13 direct reports**; range 12–18 for clinical manager roles.
- **35% of managers (about 25 of 70) were appointed in the last 18 months** during the expansion wave.
- Note: the 2022 policy was written for spans of 6–7. Every manager now manages at least 12 people.

**Analysis:**
- At a median span of 13, weekly one-on-ones of 20 minutes would consume ~4.3 hours per manager per week, plus scheduling overhead — time that does not exist on anyone's roster (scorecards below). Biweekly one-on-ones of 20 minutes ≈ 2.2 hours/week, which is feasible only if the time is rostered.
- This is an arithmetic finding, not a motivation problem: the requested structure (regular 1:1s) is impossible to schedule informally at current spans.

**Implication (environment cause):** spans of 12–18 make informal feedback structurally impossible; a cadence + template + rostered time is a prerequisite, and the span trend itself is flagged to leadership.

## Document 4: Clinic manager scorecard (operations, current year)

**What it contains (with weights):**
| Metric | Weight |
|---|---|
| Patients per day (throughput) | 40% |
| Wait time variance | 20% |
| Budget variance | 20% |
| Incident closure rate | 15% |
| Roster cost | 5% |
| **Team development / staff feedback / retention** | **0%** |

**Analysis:**
- Nothing on the scorecard references team development, feedback, one-on-ones, or retention. Interviewed managers (M1, M2, M3) all described this list accurately from memory — it governs their week.
- 90-day new-hire turnover (22%) appears nowhere, though it costs the organization an estimated $9,400 per departed new hire (HR estimate; ~17 exits in the trailing 12 months ≈ $160k/year).
- Interviews suggest managers behave rationally given this card: throughput behavior is recognized and rewarded; feedback behavior is invisible.

**Implication (motivation/consequences cause):** managers are measured on clinic throughput, not team development; giving feedback has no recognition.

## What the four documents imply together

The documents define the *system* managers work in: a review form that only collects ratings, a 1:1 policy that is a suggestion, spans that eliminate informal contact time, and a scorecard that pays for throughput. Against that system, the request — "build a 2-hour course on giving feedback" — targets the only one of the four levers the documents do *not* control: the managers' skill. See [is-it-training](../03-analysis/is-it-training.html) for what that lever can and cannot fix.
