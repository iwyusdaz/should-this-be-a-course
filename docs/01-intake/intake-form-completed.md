# Completed intake form — Brightline Health Services

> **Simulated case.** Brightline Health Services and every person named in this case study are fictional; this intake record was constructed for portfolio purposes to demonstrate the intake and reframing step of a performance consulting engagement.

## 1. Request and requester

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Date received | 2026-09-14 |
| Requester | Dana Whitfield, Director of People & Culture (HR) |
| Others involved | Marcus Oyelaran, Chief Operating Officer (approver for manager time); clinic operations directors |
| **Request, verbatim** | *"Our engagement survey says employees don't get useful feedback. Can you build a 2-hour e-learning course on giving feedback for all managers? We need it live next quarter."* |
| Format and scope asked for | 2-hour e-learning course, all 70 people managers, hosted in the LMS |
| Deadline | "Next quarter" (~11 weeks from intake) — driver: the annual engagement survey results go to the board in Q2 |

## 2. The business problem in their words

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| What is going wrong? | "Employees don't get useful feedback." (engagement survey comment theme) |
| How do they know? | Annual engagement survey; HR has not analyzed which items or which clinics drive it. Survey report available on request. |
| Who is affected? | ~830 clinic staff who report to the 70 people managers across 12 regional clinics (of ~900 staff total). |
| When did it change? | Unclear. Roughly a third of managers (35% — about 25 of 70) were promoted in the last 18 months during a rapid clinic expansion; HR suspects the wave of new managers is involved but has no data. |

> **Reframing question 1:** *"The engagement survey says a lot of things. Which item, exactly, is lowest — and for whom?"*
> → HR could not answer. The survey report shows the feedback items are the lowest-scoring cluster in the company, but no clinic or role breakdown had been done. **Finding: the request is based on an unexamined survey theme.** This justified a discovery phase before any build.

## 3. What success looks like

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| **Reframing question 2:** *"Which business result would improve if managers gave better feedback?"* | → After discussion: **90-day new-hire turnover, currently 22% (target 15%)**. Exit interview coding shows "no feedback / didn't feel supported" as a stated factor in new-hire exits. |
| Current value | 22% of new hires leave within their first 90 days (17 of 78 new hires, trailing 12 months, HRIS) |
| Target value and date | 15% within 12 months of any intervention |
| How measured | HRIS separation reports, quarterly |

## 4. Who is affected and what they do now

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Who has to do something differently? | The 70 people managers (and the leaders who set their priorities and metrics). |
| What do they do now? | See the [performance gap analysis](../03-analysis/performance-gap.html) for the evidence-based version. Baseline found during discovery: only **31% of managers** hold one-on-ones at least fortnightly, and staff rate "useful feedback in the last 2 weeks" **2.4/5**. At intake, HR's impression: "managers don't give feedback." |
| What should they do instead? | Unknown at intake — deliberately not specified. To be determined by analysis, not by the intake form. |

> **Reframing question 3:** *"What have you already tried, and what happened?"*
> → A feedback "micro-learning email series" two years ago; HR could not say whether anyone read it. No other interventions. **Finding: no prior diagnosis exists to learn from.**

## 5. What has been tried

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Previous initiatives | Email micro-learning series (2024, no completion or impact data) |
| Why it didn't stick | Unknown — no measurement existed |

## 6. Constraints and context

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Budget | "Typical course budget" (~$18k quoted for external course build) |
| Timeline | 11 weeks; driven by board reporting of engagement survey results |
| Systems | LMS (compliance-oriented, SCORM), Microsoft 365 (Outlook/Teams), annual + quarterly pulse surveys, HRIS |
| Accessibility | WCAG 2.1 AA expected for anything built; organization committed to accessible procurement |
| Off-limits | Changing clinical staffing ratios is a board-level matter; anything that delays clinic operations |

## 7. What happens if we do nothing?

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| 3 months | Engagement survey results go to the board with the feedback theme unresolved; exit interviews keep citing feedback; new-hire turnover stays near 22%. |
| 12 months | Continued turnover costs (recruitment + onboarding per new hire ≈ $9,400 fully loaded, HR estimate); engagement scores likely flat. |
| Costed? | No — turnover cost had never been calculated. First estimate produced during analysis (see [stakeholder proposal](../04-solution/stakeholder-proposal.html)). |

## 8. Access for discovery

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Interviews | Approved: 3 managers, 2 staff, 1 HR business partner (names supplied by HR) |
| Documents | Annual review form; one-on-one policy; org chart with spans of control; clinic scorecards; engagement survey report |
| Data | HRIS turnover extract; calendar audit sampled for one-on-one meetings; pulse survey (n=200) fielded by the consultant with HR's approval |
| Direct observation | Not approved (clinical settings); substitute: document review + calendar audit |

## 9. Agreement to reframe

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Confirmation given | Yes — recorded in the kickoff email: *"Understood. Analyze first; if the answer is a 2-hour course, we'll build it."* (Dana Whitfield) |
| Note | This written agreement matters: it is what makes the eventual "we recommend less training than you asked for" conversation possible. |
