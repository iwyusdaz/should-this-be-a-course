# The 30-day manager practice challenge

> **Portfolio artifact for a fictional organization.** Brightline Health Services is fictional; this challenge was constructed for portfolio purposes as the reinforcement component of the [solution blend](../04-solution/index.html).

The challenge exists because the hard part of the [blend](../04-solution/solution-blueprint.html) is not knowing what to do — it's doing it the third week, when the roster bites. For 30 days after the manager briefing, every people manager runs one micro-goal per week, checks in with a peer buddy, and tracks their own cadence. It turns the new one-on-one standard into a habit before the organization's attention moves on.

**Time cost:** ~10 minutes a week beyond the one-on-ones themselves. **Buddy pairing:** HR pairs managers across clinics in week 0 (cross-clinic pairs share less context and compare notes more honestly). **Printing:** this page is print-friendly — hand it out at the briefing.

## The four weeks

| Week | Micro-goal | Buddy check-in prompt |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Book them** | Every direct report has a fortnightly one-on-one in the calendar through week 6. Use the [one-on-one template](../04-solution/solution-blueprint.html). | "How many are booked? What almost stopped you?" |
| **2 — One specific thing** | Every one-on-one this week includes one piece of specific feedback (situation → behavior → impact → request → listen). Use the [job aid](job-aid/index.html). | "What was the hardest feedback you gave? What did they say back?" |
| **3 — The hard one** | Raise the one conversation you have been avoiding. Rehearse the opening line with your buddy first. | "What are you putting off? Say the opening sentence to me now." |
| **4 — Close the loop** | Every meeting ends with one agreed next step each and the next date booked. Review your four weeks of notes. | "What changed for your team in a month? What are you keeping?" |

## Ground rules

1. **The one-on-one is never the thing that gets sacrificed.** If a clinical emergency cancels one, it is rescheduled within 48 hours — not to "next month".
2. **No scores, no reporting upward.** The challenge is self-tracked; HR sees only aggregate completion, not contents. What managers write down is their own.
3. **Buddy calls are 10 minutes, standing appointment, same slot every week.** Miss one, book the make-up before the call would have ended.
4. **Bring the job aid to every one-on-one for 30 days.** After that, keep it where you can see it. The point is not to memorize the structure — it's to run it in the room.
5. **If a conversation surfaces something big** (a grievance, a safety concern, a resignation signal), it leaves the challenge: follow the normal HR route, and tell your buddy you're doing so.

## What "done" looks like at day 30

- Cadence: every direct report has met their manager at least twice.
- Feedback: every one-on-one contained one specific piece of feedback (the [measurement plan](../06-measure/measurement-plan.html) tracks the staff-side pulse item, not this self-report).
- The calendar shows fortnightly one-on-ones booked six weeks out.
- Managers report what the next 90 days need — which feeds the [span-of-control review](../04-solution/solution-blueprint.html) conversation.

> Why a challenge instead of "training"? Because weeks 1–4 of a new habit need structure and a witness, not content. The scenario taught the move; the challenge makes it routine; the [measurement plan](../06-measure/measurement-plan.html) says whether it worked.
