# What we won't build (and why)

> **Simulated case.** Brightline Health Services and all names and figures are fictional — constructed for portfolio purposes.

The requested 2-hour course is the headline, but it's not the only thing we're saying no to. Each of these was considered; each fails the evidence test from [discovery](../02-discovery/survey-analysis.html). Saying no to these is what protects the budget and manager time for the things that will move turnover.

## 1. The 2-hour e-learning course on giving feedback

**Why it was requested:** it's the familiar shape for a survey finding.
**Why no:** the largest gaps are structural — only 31% of managers hold fortnightly one-on-ones, spans have crept to 12–18, and the scorecard pays 0% attention to team development. A course cannot edit a form, book a meeting, or re-weight a scorecard. The skill gap that does exist is narrow and specific, and 15 minutes of consequential practice targets it better than 120 minutes of content. Two hours × 70 managers of clinical backfill is the single most expensive line item in the whole engagement, spent on the smallest cause.

## 2. A knowledge quiz or "feedback styles" assessment

**Why it's tempting:** LMS-friendly, easily reported, makes completion dashboards look busy.
**Why no:** managers can already *describe* good feedback (the interviews show it); knowledge isn't the deficit. And completion rates are not outcomes — the [measurement plan](../06-measure/measurement-plan.html) tracks cadence, behavior and turnover instead.

## 3. A communication-styles / personality module for all staff

**Why it's tempting:** broad, popular, feels like culture change.
**Why no:** no evidence points to staff-side knowledge or manager-staff *style* mismatch; the same survey shows structure and incentives, not interpersonal compatibility. It would spend goodwill and budget on a cause the data doesn't support.

## 4. A manager "training week" or offsite

**Why it's tempting:** immersive, signals seriousness.
**Why no:** 12 regional clinics cannot lose their managers for a week without patient-flow impact (the constraint is in the intake), and an event doesn't survive contact with a scorecard that rewards throughput and a calendar with no meeting time. Habits are built by the [30-day challenge](../05-build/30-day-challenge.html), not by an offsite.

## 5. An LMS deployment, SCORM packaging, completion tracking

**Why it's tempting:** "if it's not in the LMS, did it happen?"
**Why no:** the scenario is 15 minutes and runs in any browser; the job aid is a page. Wrapping them in an LMS adds weeks of packaging for compliance-style reporting that measures the wrong thing. (If the organization later requires LMS hosting for access reasons, the scenario exports cleanly — but the measurement plan never depends on it.)

## 6. Sending the feedback email series again

**Why it's tempting:** it already exists from 2024.
**Why no:** the 2024 series had a ~30% open rate and no measurable effect (HR1). Re-running an intervention that failed without changing the system around it is the definition of hope-based design.

---

**What we will build** is the [blend](index.html): the cadence and template, the form change, the span review, the job aid, the scenario, the challenge, and the measurement plan. Less training. More performance.
