# "Can you build a 2-hour e-learning course on giving feedback?"

**Short answer: no — and the analysis shows why. This is the full case study of what the analysis found instead.**

> **Simulated case.** Brightline Health Services is a fictional organization and every artifact here — intake, interviews, survey data, documents — was **constructed for portfolio purposes** to demonstrate a complete performance consulting engagement. See [Attribution](https://github.com/iwyusdaz/should-this-be-a-course/blob/main/ATTRIBUTION.md).

![Screenshot of the branching scenario: Sam's opening scene at the nurses' station, with the choice "Head over and talk to Priya"](assets/scenario-preview.png)

**Play the [branching scenario](05-build/scenario/index.html)** — the 15-minute practice activity at the heart of the recommended blend.

## The request, verbatim

> *"Our engagement survey says employees don't get useful feedback. Can you build a 2-hour e-learning course on giving feedback for all managers? We need it live next quarter."*
> — the (fictional) HR Director, [completed intake form](01-intake/intake-form-completed.html)

The familiar move is to say yes and build the course. The consulting move is to ask what the survey *actually* shows before designing anything. The [intake form](01-intake/intake-form-template.html) captures the request in the requester's own words and starts the reframing: *which business result would move if managers gave better feedback?* Answer: **90-day new-hire turnover, 22% (target 15%)**.

## Discovery: the request complicates itself

Two weeks of [interviews](02-discovery/interview-notes-simulated.html) (3 managers, 2 staff, 1 HR), a [200-person pulse survey](02-discovery/survey-analysis.html) across all 12 clinics, and a [document review](02-discovery/document-review.html) (review form, one-on-one policy, org chart, scorecards). The messy details mattered:

- An experienced manager insists he gives feedback "constantly" — his team's usefulness scores are among the worst in the company.
- HR has a one-on-one policy; it says meetings are "encouraged", defines nothing, and tracks nothing.
- One clinic (Cedar Point) meets constantly and scores **worst** of all — meetings are not the same as feedback.

## The twist: it's mostly not a training problem

Run the evidence through [Gilbert's Behavior Engineering Model](03-analysis/root-cause-bem.html) and the [Mager & Pipe flow](03-analysis/mager-pipe-flowchart.html) and four causes emerge — only one of which training can fix:

1. **Environment:** one-on-ones aren't scheduled; managers have 12–18 direct reports; no template, no rostered time.
2. **Process:** the annual review is the only formal feedback point, and the form only asks for ratings.
3. **Skill:** some managers (concentrated in the newest cohort) avoid hard conversations and can't structure feedback — *the narrow, real training need*.
4. **Motivation:** scorecards pay for throughput only; team development weighs 0%.

The killer number: feedback usefulness differs by **1.5 points** between staff with and without regular one-on-ones — but by only **0.3 points** across manager tenure. "Train the new managers" would miss most of the problem. [Is it training?](03-analysis/is-it-training.html) gives the two-minute defense.

## The solution: a blend, and a list of things that won't be built

The [action map](04-solution/action-map.html) works backwards from the turnover goal to observable manager behaviors, and each behavior gets the *cheapest intervention that works*:

- **Non-training:** a fortnightly one-on-one standard + template, a review-form change, and a span-of-control review flagged to leadership
- **Performance support:** the ["Feedback in 5 minutes" job aid](05-build/job-aid/index.html) (HTML + tagged PDF)
- **Practice:** a [15-minute branching scenario](05-build/scenario/index.html) — Sam, Priya, three decisions, three endings, consequences instead of "Correct!"
- **Reinforcement:** a [30-day practice challenge](05-build/30-day-challenge.html) with peer check-ins
- **Measurement:** a [plan](06-measure/measurement-plan.html) with leading and lagging indicators and a staggered-rollout isolation design

Equally important: [what we won't build](04-solution/what-we-wont-build.html) — the 2-hour course, the quiz, the personality module, the offsite, the LMS packaging — and why. The [stakeholder proposal](04-solution/stakeholder-proposal.html) makes the case in two pages with a cost comparison.

## Measurement, and the honesty section

The [baseline dashboard](06-measure/baseline-dashboard.html) shows where Brightline starts (31% cadence, 2.4/5 pulse, 22% turnover). The [usability test report](06-measure/usability-test-report.html) documents what was actually tested — automated scripted tests and audits — what is pending (reviewer walkthrough, AI-simulated learner runs, multi-model review panel — see [evaluation/METHODS.md](https://github.com/iwyusdaz/should-this-be-a-course/blob/main/evaluation/METHODS.md)), and what no evidence here can show: real learner experience and learning gains.

Every dataset, interview and document in this case study is simulated and labeled as such. That's not a disclaimer to scroll past — it's the scope of the claim.
